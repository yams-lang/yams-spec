# Trademark policy

Apache-2.0 grants no trademark rights — §6 says so explicitly. That tells you
what the licence does *not* give you and leaves the useful half unsaid, so:

**YAMS is not claimed as a trademark.**

You may, without asking:

- **Implement the format.** Any language, any purpose, commercial or not.
- **Name your implementation after it** — `yams-rust`, `yams.py`, `PyYAMS`,
  `libyams`. This is encouraged; it is how anyone finds it.
- **Say your software reads, writes, or conforms to YAMS**, anywhere you like.
- **Fork this package**, under a name that is yours.

A format nobody may name is a format nobody adopts. The specification is the
authority on what YAMS *is*; the name is free.

Other names — products built on this format, by anyone — are their owners'
concern and are governed wherever those products live, not here.

If you are unsure whether a use is fine, open an issue and ask.
